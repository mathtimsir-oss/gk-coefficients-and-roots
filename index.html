🛠️ B. Expressing other forms using sum & product

Once you know α + β and αβ, you can rewrite many expressions without finding α and β themselves!

3α + 3β → factor out the common factor

1. 3α + 3β = 3(α + β)
2. Replace α + β with the known sum → 3 × (sum)
Strategy: Factor common numbers, then substitute α + β or αβ.

🎯 The key identity: α² + β²

We want α² + β², but we only know α + β and αβ. How can we create the square?

(α + β)² = α² + 2αβ + β²
Expand the square
Idea: Start from (α + β)² = α² + 2αβ + β²
Trick: Subtract 2αβ from both sides (value stays the same!):
(α + β)² − 2αβ = α² + β²
Result: α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ
💡 Why add & subtract? Adding 2αβ (and later subtracting the same amount) does not change the value of the expression. It just rearranges the terms so we can see α² + β² clearly.

🔗 C. Three conditions — same meaning!

From the textbook (p.18). These three statements are completely equivalent. Click each card to highlight them all.

m and n are the roots
of the equation 4x² − x = 6
⇅
m ≠ n and
{ 4m² − m = 6
  4n² − n = 6 }
⇅
m ≠ n and
4m² − m = 4n² − n = 6
Click any card above. They all mean the same thing!

Why are they the same?

① If m and n are roots of 4x² − x − 6 = 0, then by definition each satisfies the equation: 4m² − m − 6 = 0 and 4n² − n − 6 = 0 → 4m² − m = 6 and 4n² − n = 6.
② Conversely, if both m and n satisfy 4x² − x = 6 (and m ≠ n), then they are precisely the two distinct roots of that quadratic.
③ Writing “4m² − m = 4n² − n = 6” is just a compact way of saying both equal 6.
Useful for solving: When you see “m ≠ n and 4m² − m = 4n² − n = 6”, you can immediately treat m and n as the roots of 4x² − x − 6 = 0 and use sum & product formulas!
